# English phonetics

The table below gives a practical overview of the main phonemes of **General American (GA)** English and **Standard Southern British (SSB, often called modern RP)**. Accents differ, and some speakers merge or distinguish sounds differently. The British column shows the British IPA equivalent or a notable pronunciation difference; `—` means there is no major difference for the example shown.

**Notes:** IPA symbols represent sounds, not spellings; one spelling can have different pronunciations. GA is rhotic (it pronounces /r/ in **car**, **bird**, and **teacher**), while SSB is generally non-rhotic unless /r/ is followed by a vowel. British and American vowel inventories vary by region and speaker, so the British column gives common SSB pronunciations rather than a rule for every UK accent.
